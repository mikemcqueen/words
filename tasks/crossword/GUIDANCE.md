As additional guidance, my expected interpretation of "eden woman" is Eve, and my expected interpretation
of "one wood" is a golf club. I want you to look for reasoning in the results files that considered those
interpretations, but then dismissed them for some reason. You should design a prompt that considers those
interpretations but does *not* dismiss them. So you will want to carefully consider the reasons why they
were dismissed and construct a prompt accordingly.

It is important to understand that the provided word pairs are just a small sampling; there are many more -
tens of thousands - that I'd like your prompt to work with. Therefore, you should design a prompt in as
general terms as possible - don't "cheat" by specifying any of the word pairs in your prompt, or, for example,
by specifying "Eve" or "Golf club" in your prompt. It's OK to specify broad categories, but you shouldn't
be use specific categories such as "sports equipment" or "biblical references". Remember, these word-pairs
are just a very small sample, and your prompt must work across a broad set of other unrelated word-pairs.

Many of the prompts refer to the word-pair describing or referring to something "real", and the reasoning
in some cases does not consider Eve to be "real", with the prompt failing for the word-pair "eden woman"
for that reason. The use of the word "real" here is counter-productive, and I challenge you think think
of a better word choice.

Many of the prompts are divided into a "YES" condition and a "NO" condition, such as "Answer YES if some
condition is met. Answer NO if the word combination is nonsense." Do not use this approach; instead, your
prompt should include a single YES/NO question, and a directive to reply with YES or NO, such as, "Is this
word pair meaningful? Answer with YES or NO only."
